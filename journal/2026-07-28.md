### Execution: first attempt failed, and the reason matters

The first order was **SPY 710P exp 7/31, limit $0.24** (order
`6a6901c2-796b-4f99-b9a5-4ae4cb5cf276`), placed 15:23 ET. It never filled and
was cancelled at 15:28.

That failure was structural, not bad luck. Buying power of $24.37 caps the
payable price at $0.24 ($25.04 at the $0.25 offer would have been rejected), so
the order could only ever rest *at* the bid — never cross the spread. Which
means:

> The order could only fill if sellers hit the bid, i.e. if the put was
> cheapening, i.e. if SPY was rising — the scenario in which the thesis is
> **wrong**. It would systematically fail to fill exactly when the thesis was
> working.

That is textbook adverse selection, and it is what happened: SPY ticked from
741.81 down to 741.44, the market richened from 0.24/0.25 to 0.25/0.26, and the
resting bid was stranded *below* the bid with 975+ contracts queued ahead of it.

**Lesson: when buying power caps you below the offer, a resting bid is not a
patient entry — it is a bet against your own thesis.** Either buy something you
can pay the offer for, or do not trade.
