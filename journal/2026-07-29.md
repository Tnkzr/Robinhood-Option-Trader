> ## ⚠️ THE LESSON OF THIS SESSION — read before trading again
>
> **I sold 2 SPY 731P (0DTE) at $0.03 for $6 at 2:03 PM CST. SPY was 742.03 —
> 742.59 was the high of the day. It closed at 729.46. Those puts settled worth

>
> Same error, second position: the SPY 721P stopped out at 0.18 (−$13) closed
> marked at $1.155 — it would have been **+$84**. The two exits forfeited
> roughly **$400 on a $121 account**.
>
> **The reasoning failure**: I declared the thesis "comprehensively dead" citing
> SMH recovering from −3.1% to −0.26%. SMH *closed at 504.22* — a new low,
> −4.8%. I called a structural reversal off 17 minutes of tape, against a
> three-day forced-liquidation thesis that was fully intact. The trade was
> right; the exit was the mistake.
>
> **The category error**: I applied equity-style "cut your losers" logic to
> convex instruments. A long option *already is* a stop — the premium is the
> entire risk. Selling a $0.03 contract to recover $6 surrenders 100% of the
> tail for a rounding error, and the tail is the only reason to own it.
>
> ### Rules adopted (account owner's instruction, 2026-07-29)
>
> 1. **No stop-losses on long options while the thesis is intact.** Premium paid
>    = risk budget = max loss. Size so total loss is acceptable.
> 2. **Never sell a cheap convex position for scraps.** Below ~25% of entry,
>    holding to expiry dominates: recovery is trivial, forfeited upside is not.
> 3. **A multi-day macro thesis does not die on an intraday counter-move.**
>    Invalidation needs structural evidence — a higher *close*, not a bounce.
> 4. **Stops still belong on directional trades broken by a price level.** The
>    XLE stop was correct and would be repeated: that thesis genuinely broke and
>    XLE closed lower.
>
> Accepted trade-off: this produces more total losses and uglier stretches
> before a tail pays. That is the correct structure for convex bets, provided
> sizing assumes zero. Flag positions held-to-expiry-by-design at entry.

### SPY put stop rationale — $0.10, deliberately loose

A tight stop here would be actively harmful. Theta is **−0.472/day** on a

bought for.

$0.10 sits below that decay path. Reaching it requires SPY rallying roughly
+0.5–0.7%, which genuinely damages the thesis. Caps loss near −$8 instead of the
full −$18, without stopping out on noise or on the clock.

### Why the XLE stop was different, and stays

XLE was a directional trade on a technical level: crude pulling energy equities
higher, invalidated by a decisive break below the 58.77 session low. XLE closed
at 58.575 — below the stop level. The thesis genuinely broke and the stop saved
money. That logic is sound and is retained.

The SPY puts were not that. They were convex macro bets whose entire value was
the tail, on a thesis that never broke. Applying a price stop to them converted
capped-risk optionality into realized losses immediately before the payoff.
**Different instruments require different exit logic** — that is the distinction
I collapsed today.
